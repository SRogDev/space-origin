# Value axes — the design space of civilizations

The player doesn't pick a prefab faction. They push their civilization along
these axes through policies, technologies, and choices. Axes interact with the
simulation: they shift population behavior, unlock/lock policies and techs,
shape events, and change what the AI narrates.

## 1. Liberty ↔ Authority

Who decides? Free press, open debate, and individual rights vs centralized
command, surveillance, and order. High authority: faster mobilization, more
unrest risk.

## 2. Individualism ↔ Collectivism

Who benefits? Personal achievement and private enterprise vs shared welfare
and collective goals. Shifts consumption, taxation tolerance, social cohesion.

## 3. Pacifism ↔ Militarism

How is security achieved? Diplomacy, demilitarization, and soft power vs
standing armies, deterrence, and expansion. Drives military spending and
threat perception.

## 4. Ecology ↔ Industrialism

What is nature? A garden to preserve vs a resource to exploit. Shapes
pollution, resource depletion rates, terraforming options, and events.

## 5. Secular ↔ Spiritual

What gives meaning? Rationalist, scientific worldview vs faith, tradition,
and transcendence. Influences culture, education, social movements, and
how the population interprets crises.

---

Combinations, not archetypes: a civilization can be ecologist *and* militarist,
spiritual *and* libertarian. The interesting states live in the combinations.
